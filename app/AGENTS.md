# Prototype Instructions

Run the local server yourself and open the preview in the browser available to this environment. Do not give the user server-start instructions when you can run it.

Before making substantial visual changes, use the Product Design plugin's `get-context` skill when the visual source is unclear or no longer matches the current goal. When the user gives durable prototype-specific design feedback, preferences, or decisions, record them in `AGENTS.md`.

When implementing from a selected generated mock, treat that image as the source of truth for layout, component anatomy, density, spacing, color, typography, visible content, and hierarchy.

## Durable product decisions

- The current Soundflight direction supersedes the earlier multi-theme landing composition for the default experience. Its visual truth is `../assets/concepts/soundflight-luminous.png`: black lacquer, luminous opalescent worlds, event-driven colored light, and only the minimum floating controls. The user explicitly reaffirmed this styling on 2026-07-21.
- Render the primary scene directly with Three.js. Camera orbit/pan/zoom, smooth body following, launch gestures, bloom, particles, and resonance lines belong to the WebGL scene; React owns accessible controls, drawers, status, and existing score/audio workflows.
- Brightness must communicate musical energy. Keep the resting sky dark and sparse, then intensify the selected world's opal emission, cyan/amber/magenta trail braid, particles, birth bloom, and resonance knot from real physics/audio events. Avoid permanent orbit grids and decorative noise.
- `ADD PLANET` is the single creation entry. It arms a radial gesture that starts at the star, moves outward to preview a pitch, snaps to a stable musical orbit, and releases the new voice into the score. Do not reintroduce hold-to-grow or empty-sky throwing in the primary flow. A successful release starts audio but keeps the editorial composition camera stable. Always provide visible `+` / `−` zoom controls in addition to scroll and pinch.
- Composition mode has no auto-rotation, camera following, or drag-to-fly. Zoom stays available; orbit/pan flight is an explicit secondary `EXPLORE` mode with a clear return to composition.
- Every cosmic voice has one persistent named color. Its closed physical orbit is its string; the same note event that drives Web Audio sends a light pulse around that orbit. Never draw arbitrary planet-to-planet or star-to-planet chains. A bridge may appear only for a real detected resonance and must blend the two participating voice colors. Color must always be paired with the voice label and note cue.
- Preserve deterministic physics, Kepler sonification, recorded birth/removal/pluck events, preset systems, listener replies, and share payload compatibility beneath the new surface.
- Adapt GPU load instead of silently changing the artwork: cap pixel ratio, trail samples, and particle counts by device metrics; honor reduced motion by disabling auto-drift and reducing particles while retaining the core composition.
- The accepted visual targets are concepts 1, 2, and 5: `black-lacquer-orbit.png`, `white-time.png`, and `sumi-gravity.png` in `../assets/concepts/`.
- All three are selectable presentations of one shared physical simulation and musical score. Switching theme must never change bodies, orbits, time, notes, playback position, or the shared composition payload.
- The author may save a preferred theme, while the recipient may override it locally without mutating the recording.
- Keep the primary screen sparse and full-bleed. Recording, sharing, and theme selection must not turn the experience into a dashboard.
- Balance felt play with inspectable physics: optional panels may explain or challenge, while the default scene stays an art object.
- Cosmic voices must be labelled as sonifications, never literal airborne recordings from vacuum. A voice imprint is part of the shared score and must reproduce for the recipient.
- Resonance challenges must use the real unsnapped N-body state; never auto-correct an orbit to manufacture success.
- Touching a visible world must immediately reveal its assigned voice from the current live physical state; dragging the same world changes the orbit, time flow, color, and music.
- Treat sharing as the end of the play loop, not a utility action. The dance record must carry orbit gestures, voice imprints, and earned resonance seals, and the recipient must be able to replay or answer with a new orbit.
- Mobile is a primary performance surface. Keep core touch targets at least 44 px and verify portrait, compact portrait, and landscape without document overflow.
- While `ADD PLANET` is armed, dragging outward from the star is the only birth gesture. Distance chooses one of the authored musical orbit radii, the gestation tone previews its exact Kepler pitch, and release commits it. A short or off-star gesture must surface a clear error and never silently birth a world.
- Pitch is honest Kepler sonification: a world's fundamental is its live orbital frequency raised exactly twelve octaves, so resonance ratios are heard as just intervals. The authored `frequency` field is only a fallback for bodies without a bound period.
- Births and consumptions are score events (`add-body`/`remove-body`) so a shared dance replays the universe being made. Born worlds keep their birth voice everywhere, including replies; only the core trio accepts voice imprints.
- Feeding a born world to the star is the only removal gesture; the core trio is permanent. The sky holds at most 12 worlds.
- Birth visuals follow the surface: dark themes conceive in cyan light, light themes conceive in ink. Consumption always flashes warm coral.
- The composer's dance never loops or resets on its own; only the listener replay loops at the recorded duration. Restart (↺) begins a fresh take: it clears recorded events so timestamps stay monotonic.
- Every drawn orbit is a harp string. Touching the line (not the world, not the void) plucks it at its live Kepler pitch; sweeping across lines strums them with per-string cooldown. Pluck position sets timbre (near the world dark and long, far bright), swipe speed sets strength. Gesture priority is fixed: world grab, then string pluck, then void birth.
- Plucks are recorded as `pluck` events and replay for the listener; listeners may also pluck the received harp locally without touching the record. Plucking never changes physics — strings sound the orbit, they do not push it.
- The harp shelf loads authored star systems (quinta, octava, penta, cometa) as fresh takes; presets keep the io/europa/callisto trio as the first three strings (extra strings are roster novas in the initial state), keep the player's current theme, and are hidden from listeners.
- String ripples and hover glints use the same surface rule as births: cyan light on dark themes, ink on light themes.
- The sky is real geometry, never a flat backdrop: a seeded procedural starfield (three depth behaviors: far shell, warm ecliptic dust, rare bright accents) parallaxes with the camera, and a custom PMREM environment (warm gold below, cool blue above, magenta accent) lights the opal materials. Keep `pmremGenerator.fromScene` sigma at 0.04 — larger values clip.
- Harp strings are closed osculating orbits and must stay visible while paused (dimmer), because plucking is allowed while paused. Avoid accumulated history trails and permanent orbit grids: there is exactly one string per playable body. Every string/rim/pulse color derives from `voiceVisual`; approach and recession may tint the body through `dopplerTintedColor`. No separate trail palette.
- The star acknowledges the ensemble: notes flash its corona (`starVisual.impulse`), and the visible breath phase is exported as `starBreath` on `onPhysicsFrame`, driving the audio drone's gain/filter. Keep visual breath and bass breath on this single phase.
- Audio space comes from one generated stereo convolution reverb (seeded noise, darkening tail, pre-delay); every voice and one-shot sends to it, wetter with distance from the star. Voice fundamentals use `VOICE_HARMONICS` PeriodicWaves; the master chain is sum → high-shelf tame → gentle tanh saturation → compressor. No external audio assets.
- First-run teaching is one serene overture line plus contextual whispers (grab a born world → "drag into the star to set this voice free"); never a tutorial wall. Errors are sentence-case, serif, and self-fade in ~6 s. The transport button pulses an invitation until the first play.
- Soundflight chrome uses exactly two tokens: `--sf-gold` and `--sf-accent`. Never reintroduce per-element hardcoded golds/cyans. Micro-labels never drop below 8px.
- Always guard `setPointerCapture` (see `capturePointer`) — synthetic pointers and lifted styluses throw otherwise. When a drag consumes a world, clear `runtime.latestGesture` and never flush a gesture for a body the engine no longer knows, or the recorded score becomes unshareable.
- The legacy 2D `OrbitalStage`, `ThemeChooser`, and `HarpShelf` were removed 2026-07-21 (git history keeps them); the Three.js Soundflight stage is the only renderer.
- Orbit Harp is the durable instrument model as of 2026-07-23: orbit = string, planet = primary voice, moon = overtone/subdivision, fixed light needle = note trigger, moving pulse = sounded note, and a resonance bridge = a real physical interval. These mappings must remain visually and sonically consistent.
- Planet birth uses five tuned non-overlapping orbital slots. A drag chooses the nearest free slot; when all five sound, birth fails clearly instead of stacking worlds into a visual or physical knot.
- Moon birth is a direct gesture with no arming button or persistent selection panel: pull any planet outward, reveal its Hill-stability annulus only after the drag threshold, then release inside the band. A short tap auditions the planet and creates nothing. Each planet holds at most two moons. Moons inherit the parent's voice color, orbit under the shared N-body integrator, sound at their own local Kepler period, and survive score sharing/replay. The local moon subsystem may be uniformly magnified for legibility, but the pointer conversion boundary must always map back into the true physical Hill band.
- Moon teaching must never render an idle seed, handle, annulus, or orbit around a selected planet. Before the drag begins, show no satellite geometry at all; reveal the stability band and moon preview only while the pointer is actively choosing an orbit.
- The public instrument name is `WAI GRAMOPHONE`. Keep the `/relativity` route and existing score links compatible, but never present “Relativity Gramophone” as the product title.
- A received score derives its displayed world/moon roster from its timed `add-body` and `remove-body` events, not only from the opening `bodies` snapshot. Keep those events timed so the listener still hears the universe being composed, but start visual playback immediately on open; Web Audio may unlock on the listener's first gesture.
- The deterministic birth-voice cycle includes the established voices plus theremin, Ondes Martenot, and Trautonium-inspired profiles. Their continuous glide, vibrato, subharmonic color, and persistent orbit colors must remain musically distinct without adding new UI controls.
- Composition camera stays aligned with the fixed physical trigger axis and auto-fits the live system. The default instrument does not expose separate zoom, rotation, pan, or `EXPLORE` controls; cosmic travel is a semantic transition triggered by one `FLY` action.
- The approved cosmic extension is one continuous instrument, not a separate galaxy screen. Semantic zoom crosses `STAR SYSTEM` → `MILKY WAY` → `DEEP UNIVERSE`; the camera distance mixes both the rendered scale and the audio perspective. At galactic scales, nearby orbit strings recede while the living spiral, its record grooves, choir, and distant galaxies become audible and visible.
- `LIGHT` is the dedicated gravitational theremin surface: touch and move horizontally for pitch and vertically for intensity/timbre, then return with one `BACK` action. It is monophonic and continuous. Keeping it separate from the orbital canvas makes planet birth, moon birth, orbit pluck, and the theremin mutually legible.
- Abundance must stay causal. Orbit plucks may release one memory comet toward the galactic center; strong real two-body resonance may reveal the resonance cathedral. Neither effect may run as ambient decoration, stack indefinitely, or compete with the active gesture.
- Every object beyond the player's own system is drawn from measured astronomy, never from a palette chosen by eye. A star's colour is its blackbody colour on the Planckian locus; a world's look comes from its measured radius and the starlight it actually receives; the habitable zone is drawn where the host star's luminosity puts it. Where a value is compressed to fit a screen the compression is logarithmic and order-preserving, and the surface says so. Data lives in `cosmicAtlas.js` (physics), `galaxyShape.js` (structure) and the landmark tables in `cosmicInstrument.js`.
- The Milky Way is SB(rs)bc: four named arms (Perseus, Scutum-Centaurus, Sagittarius-Carina, Norma) opening at a 12 degree pitch angle from the ends of a bar 27 degrees off the Sun-centre line, an old warm bulge, the Sun 27,000 light-years out, and Sagittarius A* at the centre. The record grooves are those real arms. Never restore the schematic three-arm field or the dark centre — a real barred spiral has a bright bulge.
- Galaxies are point clouds built from real structure: an exponential disc sampled by its exact inverse CDF (clamping instead piles a bright ring onto both edges), arm ridges whose angular width narrows outward, young blue stars on the ridge, pink HII knots where they are being born, and a sparse warm halo. Clouds are shuffled so lowering the point budget on a phone thins a galaxy evenly instead of deleting its halo and its arms. Galaxy clusters are many small galaxies of mixed morphology merged into one cloud, elliptical-dominated at the core.
- Nearby stars are laid out in the plane the camera actually reads. The neighbourhood camera looks almost straight down +z, so screen-vertical is world y and world z is only depth; a layout that spreads in z piles the systems on top of each other. Names stay small and sit directly under their own object, and a name never bleeds through from a neighbouring scale.
- Cosmic navigation is a child-readable sequence of five authored destinations: `MY SYSTEM`, `NEARBY STARS`, `MILKY WAY`, `LOCAL GROUP`, and `DEEP UNIVERSE`. `FLY` immediately moves one scale outward; `HOME` immediately returns to the system. Do not place a destination map, fine zoom, or free-camera panel over the composition.
- Each semantic map is a separate honest editorial scale, not a fake continuous physical zoom. Preserve real names, nesting, and displayed distances; explicitly keep screen positions and apparent sizes schematic so stars, galaxies, and clusters remain legible. The Milky Way must become one peer in the Local Group instead of remaining the dominant full-size backdrop.
- Every outer scale must remain playable. Show at most four named luminous landmarks at once; touching one sounds its distinct physical/futurist voice and triggers one bounded light response. Two-finger pinch always wins over a pending empty-space theremin hold, and reduced motion freezes ambient drift without removing navigation or sound.
- The default mobile HUD has at most three circular actions: sound transport, `LIGHT` or `HOME`, and `FLY`. `HOW TO PLAY`, sharing, and removal live in one overflow menu; body selection must not create persistent chrome.
- The first audio state is one centered `TOUCH TO HEAR` gate. Its trusted gesture sets the WebKit audio session to media playback before graph creation, schedules an audible source before awaiting resume, and verifies that the audio clock advances before showing `PAUSE`. Visibility recovery may resume automatically; if WebKit refuses it, return honestly to the same one-tap sound gate.
- Mobile high-DPI rendering is a product requirement. Target up to a 2x WebGL backing buffer within an explicit pixel budget; lower particle/trail density before lowering a Retina-class phone to a 1x buffer. Verify both the logical viewport and drawing-buffer scale in compact iPhone emulation.
- The composer must own a multisampled render target. A plain `EffectComposer` target resolves every line and silhouette at one sample, and that is one of the two things that read as pixelated. `selectRenderProfile` carries `samples`, and reduced motion is the only state that gives them up. `alphaToCoverage` is **not** the way to smooth an orbit string: quantising a thin line's alpha into four MSAA samples makes its brightness step as its sub-pixel position drifts, and a smooth ellipse comes out as a chain of beads (measured: 8.3% brightness ripple with it, 4.2% without, and a 39% brighter line). The multisampled target resolves the quad's own edges, so ordinary alpha blending is both smoother and brighter. Additive blending on `Line2` has the same fault for a different reason — consecutive segment caps overlap at every joint and double up.
- Nearby stars are playable destinations rather than decorative lights. A tap must enter a full-screen, uncluttered editorial system whose world count, names, orbital periods, orbital ordering, and relative radii come from primary astronomy sources. Our own Solar System is one of those destinations, so a child can study the planets they already know.
- A system is transposed into hearing by one shared whole-octave shift for the whole system, not per planet. Inside a system a slower orbit is then always a lower voice and every pitch ratio is exactly the orbital period ratio, so TRAPPIST-1's published resonance chain arrives as a real chord. Only a system too wide for the register — the Solar System spans nine octaves — has its extremes folded, and folding is always a whole number of octaves. One law carries every scale: the bigger and slower it is, the deeper it sounds.
- A Retina screen must be drawn at its real physical resolution, on the desktop as much as on a phone. `selectRenderProfile` opens at 2x within a 12-megapixel budget, which keeps a full-screen browser on a Retina laptop at a true 2x; only a canvas far larger than a laptop screen gives resolution back. What the machine can actually hold is then measured, not guessed: `nextQualityLevel` reads the median frame cost of a 90-frame window and walks `QUALITY_LADDER`, which surrenders multisampling before it surrenders resolution. A hidden tab is never measured.
- Nothing in the sky may be drawn with a hard edge. A radial falloff that merely reaches zero still leaves a kink in its own derivative, and on an absolutely black sky the eye reads that kink as a drawn circle; every glow texture lands on zero with a zero slope, and the two widest star skirts reach exactly zero outside the system scale rather than a small number. Never bake rings, arcs or other geometry into a glow texture.
- Every named field is laid out in the frame the camera actually has. `landmarkPlacement` stretches authored unit slots onto the visible half-width and half-height, so six nearby stars or four galaxies fill a 16:10 laptop and a portrait phone equally instead of huddling into the middle of one; a portrait frame also starts the field lower, because a third of its height is title and instruction. Anything derived from those positions — the cosmic web's filaments — is rebuilt when the layout changes.
- A visited system is a thing you read, not a thing you compose in. Its camera opens further (`visitedSystem`, about 38 degrees) so seven concentric orbits separate into rings a child can aim at, and its distance is solved rather than guessed (`visitedSystemCameraDistance`) so every world and its name stay inside a portrait frame.
- Touching one world of a visited system sounds that world alone, at exactly the pitch it contributes to its system's chord (`systemWorldVoice`); touching the star sounds the whole system. Every world carries its own name, its real year and its class, and the worlds are large enough to aim at and named legibly at whatever distance the camera had to stand back to.
- A star is a photosphere, not a disc of colour: Eddington limb darkening, granulation, and a white core only in proportion to how hot the star actually is, so a 2566 K dwarf stays orange instead of blowing out to white. A world is lit by its own star and nothing else: a soft terminator, a night side holding only a trace of its own colour, and a Fresnel atmosphere that brightens on the lit limb. Both stay inside their own colour rather than clipping, and let the corona and the bloom carry the brightness.
- Everything the player's own composition draws belongs to the player's own system and must vanish with it. A resonance bridge or an orbit string left on at galactic scale hangs across the Milky Way as ghost geometry from a scale the player has left. A resonance bridge is a standing wave pinned at both ends, and it needs enough samples per period to be a wave rather than a staircase.
- Among its family our galaxy is a peer, not the backdrop: its drawn radius says 26.8 kpc against Andromeda's 46.6, and every Local Group galaxy's drawn radius is the square root of its real stellar diameter — one stated compression, order- and ratio-preserving. Our galaxy stands where its own name stands.
- Sound that is already running is never started again. Re-running the gesture handshake puts its clock-verification probe in front of every single note, and an instrument has to answer immediately.
- Pause holds everything, including the worlds of a visited system. Worlds that keep circling under a paused transport make the button read as broken.
- The canvas is inert exactly while the `TOUCH TO HEAR` gate covers it — keyed off `data-audio-state="locked"`, never off an onboarding step. A lesson step is not a statement about what the player may touch: keying it there made pausing kill every touch on the canvas, including the worlds of a system the player had travelled to.
- Adaptive quality is measured against the display, not against a fixed millisecond count. A browser paces `requestAnimationFrame` to vsync, so a machine comfortably keeping up still reports 16.7 ms on a 60 Hz screen; an absolute recovery threshold below that is unreachable and the artwork stays dimmed forever after one slow moment. The window's fastest frame is the refresh interval, keeping up means the median sits on it, and recovery asks for several good windows in a row while one bad window is enough to give ground.
- A struck world's highlight fades on real time even when the transport is paused or reduced motion has frozen the orbits. Decay is a response, not orbital motion, and freezing it leaves a world permanently lit.
- The instrument opens with worlds already turning. A star alone is not an instrument: it has no strings to pluck, no orbits to touch and nothing to hear, and a first frame like that is fairly read as "there is effectively only one planetary system". `createBlankComposition` deletes the core trio this file calls permanent; it is not the opening state.
- Slower is always deeper, in every system, without exception — and that beats exact intervals when the two conflict. Folding each voice back into the register one octave at a time is not order-preserving and inverted THE SUN, the system a child opens first. A system moves as a block: whole octaves where its span fits the register, so a real resonance still arrives as a real interval, and one shared logarithmic compression where it cannot. A test asserts a strictly falling chord for every system in the catalogue.
- A ring passing behind its star is the strongest depth cue a picture of a planetary system has. A visited system that is fully present is opaque and writes depth. But light in front of a body is still light: every additive glow, corona and halo keeps `depthTest: false` and an explicit `renderOrder`, or the depth writes clip the very skirts they belong to and each soft glow becomes a hard-edged annulus on its own silhouette.
- A world's drawn size is a stated power of its real radius against the largest world in its own system. An additive floor plus a cube root squeezed the Solar System's true 29:1 spread into 1.97:1, so a gas giant and a rock arrived the same size and nothing about a world's size meant anything.
- Touch targets never draw themselves. An untextured sprite is a solid quad however low its opacity, and linear 0.001 survives the tone curve at about 3/255; hit areas carry `colorWrite: false`, which raycasting does not read.
- Every label is mipmapped and anisotropically filtered at the GPU's real maximum. Names are shown three to four times minified, and without a mipmap chain that is point sampling — a serif stem lands as a hard on/off column. `anisotropy` without mipmaps does nothing. Text is the one thing on screen whose correct appearance the eye already knows, so it is the reference against which everything else is graded.
- The nearby sky is four shells of increasing distance, not one shelf. A sky stops being readable somewhere around seven or eight named lights, so more content arrives as more skies: `FLY` steps one shell further out before it leaves for the galaxy, every press is honestly "further from home", and each shell is a ring with the middle left clear for the player's own star. Out there the player's star is an anchor, not a system: its worlds, strings and earned resonances belong to the system scale.
- Star systems come from the NASA Exoplanet Archive (`pscomppars`), generated into `starSystems.js`; only a display name and a one-line lesson are editorial. A system with one body has a one-note chord and is not a system. A system is built the first time the sky it belongs to is looked at, because a whole catalogue constructed at boot for a room nobody opens is how a universe stops fitting on a phone.
- A gesture that does not complete says so. A short pull on the star used to return literal silence, which reads as a broken instrument rather than an unfinished gesture.
- A journey between scales must end however it ends. FLY takes a few seconds and the stars it flies towards are tappable the whole way, so touching one supersedes the flight — and the flight can then never report that it settled. Both FLY and HOME are disabled while a journey is in flight, so a superseded journey that never closed left the player inside a system, reading "FLYING TO NEARBY STARS", with no way out but a page reload. Every ending is named in `cosmicJourney.js`: settled, superseded, manual, expired, reset.
- A swipe is a path, not a point. "SWIPE IT LIKE A STRING" means a hand that lands on empty sky and sweeps across three orbits strums three orbits, so a strum arms wherever it starts — an empty-sky drag moves no camera in composition, so it was a gesture doing nothing. And each move tests the segment travelled since the last sample (`stringsAlongSweep`), never the sample alone: at 60 Hz a quick flick reports points hundreds of pixels apart, which jumps clean over every line between them and makes the fastest, most natural strum the silent one. A tap on a string still waits for the release so a second finger can cancel it; a sweep sounds as it happens unless a second finger really is down.
- The lesson teaches what the player has not done, never what the scene happens to look like. Conditioning the creation step on an empty sky silently deleted it the day the instrument began opening with three worlds already turning — so the one thing the owner asks for first, making planets and moons, was never mentioned to anybody. Four steps: hear it, play a string, make a world, give it a moon.
- The picture is measured, not argued about: ink coverage, tonal range and clipping, banding plateaus, hue count, edge ripple. The measurements are pure functions over pixels in `frameBeauty.js` with their own tests; `frameBeautyProbe.js` hands them the live drawing buffer and exists only in development (`window.__rgBeauty`). Read the drawing buffer, never a screenshot — a screenshot of a bright object on black is resampled on its way out and invents faint rings around every star. Ink coverage is the metric that must go DOWN as the instrument improves.
- Adding your own world to a real system works, and until 2026-07-27 it never had. Three independent faults stacked: the press was claimed by the landmark audition before the birth branch could see it, so the gesture could not begin; the reach was measured against the world's `y = 0` plane while a visited system sits at the height its name occupies on screen; and the grip was measured on that plane at all, when a system is a flat disc seen from about 38 degrees above it — a press aimed squarely at the Sun landed 4.5 units out against a limit of 0.95. A player aims at what they can see, so the grip is now screen pixels around the star's projection (`starGripRadius`, never below 44), the reach is measured on the system's own plane, and a press that never became a drag still sounds the whole chord.
- The resonance cathedral answers a lock being **won**, not a lock being true. The instrument opens on periods of 10.8, 16.2 and 24.3 days — ratios of exactly 1.5 — so the resonance strength is 1.0 from the first frame of the first session and never lets go: seven arches vaulted over the composition permanently, and because the same level dims the starfield by up to half, a reward designed to be rare was holding the whole sky about 28 per cent dark. `nextCathedralLevel` fires on the crossing and decays to nothing over four seconds; a system already locked when the player arrived earned nothing and lights nothing.
- Grain rides the light, never the dark. `grain * (0.18 + luma)` kept a floor of noise alive on true black, and because ACES clamps the negative half it could only ever add: it lifted a quarter of the frame off zero and made 57 per cent of a *paused* frame change every 16 ms. Black lacquer means the black is black.
- One law carries every scale, and it has to hold in two directions at once. WITHIN a system it always did: the system moves as a block, and a uniform shift in log frequency is a transposition, so every measured ratio survives it and TRAPPIST-1's resonance chain arrives as the real chord it is. BETWEEN systems it did not hold at all — every system was re-centred on one shared anchor, which is exactly the operation that deletes how slow the system is. Measured across the catalogue the rank correlation between mean period and chord centre was **+0.44** where it must be −1: HR 8799, whose worlds take 150 years, sang higher than Kepler-80, whose worlds take four days, and ten systems too wide for the register were each stretched to fill it exactly and so ran between the identical two notes. A system's geometric-mean voice is now placed on a stated two-octave ladder (110–440 Hz) by its own mean period — half a day at the top, two hundred years at the bottom — and the system is fitted around that place, keeping its intervals whole wherever the register allows and compressing by one shared logarithmic factor where it cannot. The ladder wins, because the ladder is the law. Tests assert the correlation, that no two systems share a chord, and that every system compresses by one factor rather than per interval.
- A chord voice is sounded by its own pitch, never by its position in the list. The innermost world is the fastest and therefore the highest note, and keying loudness and brightness to the index gave it both the loudest peak and the darkest filter — a 980 Hz lowpass on a 1700 Hz fundamental removes the fundamental altogether. Loud muffled treble over quiet bright bass, in all twenty-eight systems. `systemChordVoicing` opens the cutoff above each voice's own fundamental and closes it towards it, and tilts the peak gently down with pitch.
- The one thing a place has that nowhere else has is what makes it worth travelling to, and it must be rendered. Thirty-six such lines are authored in the data — one per system and one per outer landmark — and for a long time not one of them had ever reached the screen: a child flew to TRAPPIST-1, heard a chord, read a distance already printed under the star on the way in, and had no reason to go to a second system. A visited system carries its fact under its name until the player has touched a world for themselves, and touching any outer landmark announces its fact rather than repeating the label already beside it.
- A radius nobody measured is forecast by a law with its own stated domain, and the domain is the point. `radiusFromMass` is Chen & Kipping (2017), ApJ 834, 17 — a broken power law whose exponent turns *negative* above about 132 Earth masses, because past that a planet is squeezed by its own gravity and ten Jupiter masses is barely wider than one. Reading the Neptunian branch twenty times past that bound gave HD 10180 c, d and g radii of 85, 95 and 97 Earth radii against a star 121 Earth radii across: three worlds each drawn nearly as wide as the sun they orbit. A test now asserts no catalogue world exceeds 25 Earth radii or 60 per cent of its own star — a giant around an M-dwarf really does reach about 42 per cent, so the bound is physics, not tidiness.
- A touch target is solved against the camera, never baked when the object is built. A visited system is fitted to the frame, so the same world is a different number of pixels across on a phone and on a laptop — measured on a 390 by 844 portrait phone, all 131 catalogue worlds projected targets between 15 and 42 pixels, and not one reached the 44 this instrument asks of anything a finger must hit. `systemTouchRadius` takes the camera's pixels-per-world-unit and grows the invisible sphere to clear 44 px, capped at 46 per cent of the gap to the nearer neighbouring orbit — two worlds that both claim the same pixel are worse than one that is small.
- A visited system is read from an angle chosen for the frame it is in. The system is a flat disc and the fit solves for its width, so the elevation alone decides how much of the picture it occupies: at the authored 38 degrees a portrait phone gave it 85 per cent of the width and 24 per cent of the height — a band floating in an empty column. `visitedSystemElevation` opens from 38 degrees on a laptop to 62 on a phone held upright, and never goes overhead, because a disc seen from directly above is a diagram.
- Beyond the planets, the thing you travel into is a star cluster. There are no catalogued exoplanets in another galaxy and no honest way to invent some, so the outer skies used to be four or five lights you could touch for a note and nothing you could enter. A cluster removes that cliff without inventing anything: it is already a system in exactly the sense this instrument means — a central mass with things going round it at measured radii — so it is built into the same shape a planetary system has and every gesture already written works inside one. Members are read at seven radii from core to edge, each standing for the population at that depth; the period at each radius follows from the Plummer (1911) enclosed mass and Kepler's third law, which is the same law every planet here already obeys. What changes is the scale of time — a planet's year is days, a star's orbit round the centre of a globular is millions of years — so the ladder puts a globular cluster at the bottom of the register without being told. `starClusters.js` carries the measured mass, half-light radius, distance and source for each. A fifth shell, THE OLD CLUSTERS, holds seven globulars between 6,000 and 34,000 light-years.
- A galaxy is entered through its rotation curve. Nobody has catalogued a planet in Andromeda and inventing one is out of the question, but a galaxy is already a thing that turns and how fast it turns at each radius has been measured for a century — that is a year, and a year is a pitch. The physics is deliberately *not* Kepler's: a galaxy's rotation curve is flat, so the period is the circumference over that speed and its chord is an arithmetic ladder rather than a Keplerian one. It sounds different from a planetary system because it is different. `galaxyRotation.js` carries the measured rotation speed, optical radius and source for the four Local Group landmarks; the Sun's own lap comes out at 219 million years, which is the number everybody quotes.
- One ladder holds every timescale the instrument knows, from half a day to a billion years. Stopping it at two hundred years pinned every cluster and every galaxy onto the same bottom rung. Planetary systems now use about eight tenths of an octave of the ladder, clusters sit below them and galaxies below those — a galaxy is the deepest voice there is, and tests assert each tier sings below the one inside it.
- A galaxy cluster is entered by its crossing time, and the surface says so. A cluster is bound but it does not turn: its galaxies are on long plunging orbits in every direction at once, and what has actually been measured is the spread of their speeds. Calling that a year would be a stretch, and this instrument does not stretch — so the duration is `t = 2R/sigma`, the standard timescale of any virialised system, and every label reads "TO CROSS". The cosmic web is deliberately NOT enterable: filaments and voids are not bound, have no virial radius and no crossing time, and inventing one for them is exactly what this refuses. The web stays a light you can touch, and that is the edge of the instrument.
- Sound the browser took away is not sound that never started. Switching to another tab suspends the audio context, and reporting that as `locked` put a full-screen "TOUCH TO HEAR · ONE TAP STARTS THE UNIVERSE" over a universe the player had already made — and because the canvas is deliberately inert behind that gate, it took away every gesture at once. A player who could not make a planet, could not make a moon and could not touch an orbit was hitting one bug, not three. `locked` now means only that this instrument has never sounded; after it has, a suspended context is `suspended`, the canvas stays alive, and the next thing touched starts the sound on its way to doing what was asked.
- Past the outermost measured orbit the logarithmic map has nothing left to compress and runs away with itself: in the Solar System, dragging 28 per cent beyond Neptune's ring landed a world at 157 AU — five times further out for a quarter more finger travel — and then stopped dead at a clamp. The tail is linear instead, running evenly from the outermost measured orbit to eight times it, and the visited-system camera fits the furthest world actually drawn rather than the authored rim, so a world put properly far away stays in the picture.
- A moon can be hung on any world of any system you travel to, and its year is that world's own. The catalogue therefore carries a measured mass for every world and every star: 124 of the 131 worlds have one in the archive, the seven transit-only Kepler-90 worlds have theirs forecast from radius by the invertible branches of Chen & Kipping, and each says which it is. A mass is only taken when the world's own size could hold it — the archive's row for Kepler-80 f gives 4,459 Earth masses in a body 1.21 Earth radii across, a density two and a half thousand times Earth's, and a test now refuses anything outside 0.01 to 30 times Earth's density.
- The band a moon may live in is bounded below by the world itself and above by a third of its Hill radius, and some worlds have no band at all: 55 Cancri e goes round its star in eighteen hours and its grip barely passes its own surface, so the gesture refuses out loud rather than inventing a moon inside a planet. At the other end, HR 8799 b is five Jupiter masses at sixty-eight astronomical units, its grip reaches seven AU, and a moon out there really would take fifty-five years. Both are facts about those worlds, and the instrument says both.
- A press on a world of a visited system is not yet a tap: it waits for the hand. Let go where you pressed and the world sounds its year; pull outward and you are hanging a moon on it, the same gesture as at home in a sky you travelled to.
